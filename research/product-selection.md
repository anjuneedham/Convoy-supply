## Read this before any number below

**Every landed cost in this document is an assumption.** They are reasoned estimates for cut-and-sew nylon soft goods at low MOQ, not quotes. They exist so the unit-economics model has inputs, and they must be replaced with real quotes from at least three suppliers before inventory is ordered.

`finance/unit-economics.md` is built as formulas precisely so that replacing these numbers recalculates the business rather than invalidating the plan.

**Landed cost** here means: unit cost + inbound freight + duty + inspection, per unit, delivered to us. It does not include outbound shipping to the customer, which is modeled separately.

---

## The catalog logic

These are not five products. They are **one system, sold in pieces.**

A driver's cab has four zones with a storage problem, and one SKU addresses each:

| Zone | Problem | SKU |
|---|---|---|
| Seat back / passenger seat | Daily-access items have no home | **CS-01 Cab Command Organizer** (hero) |
| Bunk wall / overhead | Vertical space entirely unused | **CS-02 Bunk Shelf Net** |
| Paperwork | Logs, BOLs, permits, receipts get destroyed | **CS-03 Manifest Folio** |
| Under-bunk / storage bay | Bulk storage is a pile, not a system | **CS-04 Haul Bag** |
| *(gated)* Dash power | Cables everywhere, not enough ports | **CS-05 Dash Power Rail** |

That structure is why the bundle works. "Buy the other three and your cab is done" is a real argument, not a discount gimmick.

# Product 1 — CS-01 Cab Command Organizer (HERO)

**The seat-back command center for the passenger seat of a sleeper cab.**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Problem solved** | Every daily-use item — phone, tablet, water, snacks, gloves, flashlight, paperwork, meds — lives loose on the passenger seat, the dash, or the floor |
| **Target customer** | OTR driver, primary avatar (see `customer-avatar.md`) |
| **Suggested selling price** | **$54.99** |
| **Est. landed cost** | **$15.50 [A]** |
| **Est. gross profit** | **$39.49** |
| **Gross margin** | **71.8%** |
| **Est. dimensions / weight** | ~22"H × 15"W × 4"D deployed; folds flat. **~1.6 lb [A]** |
| **Shipping** | Poly mailer or small box, ships as soft goods. **Low cost, low damage risk, no fragility** |
| **Competitor pricing** | Cab organizers observed around **$75.99** at specialty retail; most trucker accessories **under $70** **[V4]** |
| **Positioning** | Priced **below** specialty retail, **above** Amazon generics. Justified by build spec and design, not by claiming to be cheapest. |
| **Risk level** | **LOW** — no sizing, no electronics, no fragility, universal fit |

### Main selling angle
**Before/after.** A chaotic passenger seat next to an organized one. This is the single highest-leverage static image available to this brand and it is the hero image everywhere: homepage, landing page, ad set one, Marketplace listing one.

### Secondary angles
1. **"Everything within arm's reach"** — ergonomics without leaving the seat
2. **"Stop hunting for things"** — time/frustration
3. **"Loose items are projectiles"** — safety under hard braking
4. **"Your office deserves better"** — professional identity
5. **"Built for a Class 8, not a minivan"** — comparison against consumer car organizers

### Objections and answers
- *"Will it fit my seat?"* → Adjustable strap system, published dimensions, named compatibility list (Freightliner Cascadia, Peterbilt 579, Kenworth T680, Volvo VNL, International LT)
- *"Will it hold weight without sagging?"* → Published denier and stitch spec; tested load per pocket
- *"$55?"* → Bundle path, plus a cost-over-lifespan frame

### Bundle role
Anchor of both bundles. Never discounted alone.

### Channel potential
- **Marketplace:** Good. $54.99 is above the $10–$50 sweet spot **[V1]** — test a Marketplace-specific price of **$49.99** and measure.
- **Static post:** Exceptional
- **Meta ads:** Exceptional — carries at least 5 distinct angles

# Product 5 — CS-05 Dash Power Rail (GATED TEST SKU)

**Fused 12V + USB-C power rail with integrated cable management.**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Problem solved** | Not enough ports; cables snaking across the dash and into the footwell |
| **Suggested selling price** | **$44.99** |
| **Est. landed cost** | **$16.00 [A]** |
| **Est. gross profit** | **$28.99** |
| **Gross margin** | **64.4%** — lowest in the catalog |
| **Risk level** | **HIGH — the only high-risk SKU here** |

### Why this SKU is gated

It is the product drivers most consistently want and the one most likely to hurt us. It is an **electrical product in a vehicle**:

- Failure modes include heat, short, and fire. That is a liability surface none of the other SKUs have.
- Return and defect rates on cheap 12V electronics are structurally higher than on soft goods.
- Footwell cable routing edges toward "installation," which C5 in `niche-analysis.md` tells us to avoid.
- Margin is the worst in the range, so it absorbs risk while contributing least.

### Gate conditions — ALL must be true before this SKU is listed

1. Supplier provides **UL or ETL certification** (or equivalent recognized mark) for the exact SKU — not for "a similar model"
2. Overcurrent protection is **fused and documented**, with the fuse rating published on the PDP
3. Product liability insurance is in force
4. **First 30 days are complete and the soft-goods hypothesis is validated** — we do not take electrical risk to prove a business that has not proven itself
5. Copy contains **no installation instructions** and no claim about vehicle electrical compatibility beyond "12V accessory socket"

**If any condition fails, this SKU does not launch, and the catalog is four products.** Four coherent products beat five with a liability in the middle. `execution/decisions.md` records this as DEC-004.

## Bundles

Bundle discounts are real savings against the real sum of the individual prices. No inflated compare-at prices are used anywhere.

### The Cab Reset Kit — $89.00
CS-01 + CS-02 + CS-03 · List sum **$112.97** · **Save $23.97 (21%)**

| | |
|---|---|
| Landed **[A]** | $28.80 |
| Gross profit | $60.20 |
| Gross margin | **67.6%** |

The primary conversion target. AOV of $89 against a hero AOV of $54.99 is a **62% lift**, and it is the single most effective lever available for surviving a high CPA.

| | |
|---|---|

Expected to be a minority of orders. It exists partly to make the Cab Reset Kit read as the sensible middle choice — a deliberate and disclosed pricing structure, not a trick.

## What we deliberately did NOT include

Recorded so these get re-argued with evidence, not re-discovered by accident:

| Rejected | Why |
|---|---|
| Fridges / coolers | Heavy, expensive to ship, electrical, high return rate |
| Mattress toppers | Sizing complexity, bulk, hygiene returns |
| Inverters | Electrical + genuine fire risk + installation |
| Chrome / cosmetic accessories | Different buying motivation; fitment nightmare |
| Apparel | Sizing, inventory depth, zero relation to the core problem |
| CB / electronics | Regulated, technical support burden |
| Anything with a health or safety claim | C5 |
