## How to read this document

Every claim is tagged:

- **[V]** — Verified. Sourced from a citation listed at the bottom of the section.
- **[H]** — Hypothesis. Reasoned from verified inputs, but not itself measured. Testable.
- **[A]** — Assumption. Not verified and not strongly reasoned. Must be replaced with real data (usually a supplier quote or a live ad test) before money is committed.

**No fabricated statistics appear in this document.** Where a number would normally be quoted, and we do not have it, the document says so.

## 0. Hard constraints this brand must satisfy

These come from the operating model, not from preference. Any niche that fails one of these is out.

| # | Constraint | Why |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Value must be communicable in a **still image** | Our creative pipeline is static posts + AI imagery, not video production |
| C2 | Catalog of **3–5 SKUs** must feel complete, not arbitrary | A 4-SKU store only works if the 4 SKUs are one solution, not four products |
| C3 | Must work on **Facebook Marketplace** | Marketplace shipped items move fastest at **$10–$50** **[V1]** |
| C4 | Must work on **Meta paid** at small budget | Needs an obvious visual problem and multiple distinct angles |
| C5 | **Low operational risk** — no sizing, no fragility, no regulated claims, no bulky freight | Solo operator, no returns infrastructure |
| C6 | Must not collide with an existing trademark | See the naming conflict below |

### C6 is not theoretical — read this first

**Convoy Supply Ltd.** is a real, established company: a building-envelope / building-materials wholesale distributor founded in **1972**, headquartered in Surrey, British Columbia, operating across Canada, the Pacific Northwest US, and Maryland, at `convoy-supply.com`. **[V2]**

Separately, **Convoy Inc.** was a large US digital freight brokerage that ceased operations in 2023. **[A — recalled, not verified in this research pass; verify before relying on it]**

**Implications, and they are binding:**

1. **Any niche in construction, roofing, insulation, siding, or contractor jobsite supply is disqualified.** That is a direct head-on collision with an incumbent of the same name in the same category. This removes what would otherwise have been a strong candidate (Niche D below) and the removal is on legal grounds, not commercial ones.
2. The chosen niche should be in a category where "Convoy Supply" is *evocative* (road, haul, moving freight) rather than *descriptive of the incumbent's trade*.
3. **Before any money is spent on logo, packaging, or domain**, run a USPTO TESS search in the relevant Nice classes and a domain/handle availability check. This is a Day 1 task in `execution/30-day-plan.md`, and it is a gate, not a checkbox.

## Niche A — Long-haul / OTR truck driver cab living

**One line:** The sleeper cab is a 60-square-foot apartment that ships with zero storage. Sell the storage.

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Target customer** | Over-the-road company drivers and owner-operators, US, avg. age **47** (owner-ops **56**) **[V3]** |
| **Market size** | **~3.0–3.1M** truck drivers in the US; **~2.1M** heavy/tractor-trailer specifically; **587,000** self-employed owner-operators, of which **351,000** long-distance **[V3]** |
| **Mean wage** | **$59,710** annual mean for heavy/tractor-trailer, May 2025 **[V3]** |
| **Core problem** | Everything the driver owns lives in a space with no shelves, no drawers, and no closet. Gear slides, gets lost, gets sat on, falls during braking. |
| **Example products** | Seat-back organizers, bunk nets, under-bunk bags, 12V power/cable management, document folios, cab trash systems |
| **Typical selling prices** | Cab organizers observed around **$75.99** at specialty retail; most individual trucker accessories sit **under $70** **[V4]** |
| **Est. product cost** | **[A]** $8–$22 landed for soft-goods organizers at 50–100 unit MOQ. *Requires supplier quotes.* |
| **Potential margin** | **[H]** 65–75% gross on a $49.99 soft good at ~$14 landed |
| **Competition** | Amazon (deep, generic, unbranded) **[V4]**; specialty truck-stop retail (TRUCKiD, RADCO, TruckNTow) **[V4]**. Almost no *brand* — the category is a sea of white-label listings. |
| **Static-content potential** | **Excellent.** Before/after of a messy cab vs. an organized cab is the single most powerful static format available to any of these niches. |
| **Marketplace potential** | **Moderate-good.** Auto parts & accessories is a named major Marketplace category **[V1]**. Price band fits if SKUs sit $30–$50. Risk: truckers are mobile, so local pickup is weak; must rely on shipping-enabled listings. |
| **Meta ad potential** | **Good.** Problem is visually obvious, occupational identity is strong, and multiple angles exist (organization, safety, comfort, "your office"). |
| **Organic potential** | **Strong.** Trucking has an unusually engaged Facebook Group culture and a strong identity ("professional driver," not "truck driver"). |
| **Bundle potential** | **Excellent.** A "cab reset" kit is a natural, non-arbitrary bundle. |
| **Upsell potential** | **Good.** Low-cost folio/pouch as a $20–$29 add-on. |
| **Repeat purchase** | **[H] Moderate.** Soft goods wear out on a 1–3 year cycle; drivers also re-buy on truck changes. Not consumable. |
| **Main risks** | (1) Reaching drivers efficiently — they are on the road, not browsing. (2) Amazon Prime is the default for this buyer. (3) Owner-op median age 56 skews Facebook-native, which helps, but skews away from Instagram. |
| **Main opportunity** | The category has **products but no brand.** Every listing is a generic beige rectangle with a stock photo. A brand with a point of view and real photography wins on differentiation alone. |
| **Confidence** | **Medium-high** on the problem, **medium** on the acquisition economics, **low** on landed cost until quoted. |

**Sources:** V1, V3, V4 (see Sources section).

## Niche B — Overlanding / vehicle-based camping storage & recovery

**One line:** Weekend adventure vehicles, kitted out.

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Target customer** | 28–48, SUV/truck owners, discretionary income, identity-driven purchases |
| **Market signal** | Overlanding market growing **~14.5% annually** (Grand View Research, as cited); outdoor recreation past **$30B** in 2026 **[V5]** |
| **Core problem** | Gear chaos in the back of the vehicle; getting stuck; power away from the grid |
| **Example products** | Recovery boards, drawer systems, roof-rack bags, portable jump starters, solar fans |
| **Typical selling prices** | Traction mats observed **$43.90–$49.99**; jump starters **~$99.95–$124.95** **[V6]** |
| **Est. product cost** | **[A]** Wide spread; recovery boards are bulky and expensive to freight |
| **Potential margin** | Outdoor gear dropship margins typically **25–40%** **[V5]** — notably *worse* than Niche A's soft goods |
| **Competition** | **Heavy and brand-led.** ARB, MaxTrax, Front Runner, Decked. Buyers are brand-loyal and forum-educated. |
| **Static-content potential** | **Excellent** — this is the most photogenic niche on the list. |
| **Marketplace potential** | **Weak for the good SKUs.** Bulky gear is explicitly better suited to local pickup, not shipping **[V1]**, and the strong SKUs are >$50. |
| **Meta ad potential** | Good, but you are bidding against well-funded brands for the same lifestyle audience. |
| **Bundle / upsell** | Good |
| **Main risks** | **Bulk and freight cost.** Premium/overlanding equipment is explicitly called out as bulky, expensive to ship, and complicated to return **[V5]**. Brand-loyal buyers. Thin margins. |
| **Main opportunity** | High AOV, passionate audience |
| **Confidence** | **High** that the market is real; **high** that it is a bad fit for *this* operating model. |

**Verdict: fails C3 and strains C5.** Great niche, wrong constraints.

## Niche C — Vehicle emergency preparedness (winter / roadside)

**One line:** The kit you buy in October and hope you never open.

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Target customer** | Cautious drivers, parents buying for teen drivers, adult children buying for aging parents, snow-belt commuters |
| **Core problem** | Fear of being stranded — cold, dark, no signal, no help |
| **Example products** | Roadside kits, traction mats, jump starters, shovels, blankets |
| **Typical selling prices** | Roadside kit **$34.99** (from $49.99); traction mats **$43.90**; jump starter **$99.95** **[V6]** |
| **Est. product cost** | **[A]** Kits are assembly-heavy; margin gets eaten by component count |
| **Potential margin** | **[H]** Weak-to-moderate. A 12-component kit at a $34.99 street price has very little room. |
| **Competition** | Extremely crowded on Amazon with aggressive discounting already visible in the street pricing **[V6]** |
| **Static-content potential** | **Very good.** Fear-based problem imagery and flat-lay kit shots both work. |
| **Marketplace potential** | Good — price band fits perfectly **[V1]** |
| **Meta ad potential** | **Good but seasonal.** Peak is a ~10-week window (Oct–Dec). |
| **Bundle potential** | Excellent — the product *is* a bundle |
| **Repeat purchase** | **Poor.** One purchase per vehicle per several years. |
| **Main risks** | **Severe seasonality** (a 30-day test starting in late September lands on the leading edge, which is lucky, but the model is fragile). **Safety-adjacent claims** — "this will keep you safe" is a liability surface. Price war already in progress. |
| **Main opportunity** | Gifting motion in Q4 |
| **Confidence** | **High** that this converts seasonally; **high** that it is a poor year-round brand foundation. |

**Verdict: viable but structurally fragile.** Runner-up. Worth revisiting as a **seasonal SKU inside** a broader brand rather than as the brand itself.

## What the matrix is actually saying

Niche A does not win because it is the most exciting market. Niche B is a more exciting market. Niche A wins because it is the only candidate that clears **every** hard constraint simultaneously:

- It is the only one with **both** high margin headroom **and** Marketplace-compatible price points. B has neither. C has the price points but not the margin.
- It is the only one with a genuine **brand vacuum**. Every other niche has established DTC brands the customer already trusts.
- It is the only one where the **brand name does work for us** rather than against us.
- Its main weakness — reaching a mobile audience — is an **acquisition** problem, which is exactly what a 30-day paid test is designed to answer. The other niches' weaknesses (freight cost, seasonality, trademark, brand incoherence) are structural and no amount of testing fixes them.

**A test should be designed so that the thing most likely to kill it is the thing you are testing.** Niche A satisfies that. Niche B's killer (freight economics) would be discovered after we had already bought inventory.

## What this document does NOT establish

Stated plainly, so nobody treats this as more than it is:

1. **That truck drivers will buy from an unknown brand on Facebook.** Unproven. This is the central hypothesis of the entire test.
2. **Actual landed product cost.** Every cost figure here is **[A]**. Supplier quotes are Day 4–5 work and every margin number downstream is provisional until they land.
3. **Actual CPM/CPA for this audience.** Benchmarks exist (see `finance/unit-economics.md`) but benchmarks are not our numbers.
4. **Whether Marketplace shipping-enabled listings get meaningful impressions for this category.** Assumed from category presence; not measured.

These four unknowns are the test.
