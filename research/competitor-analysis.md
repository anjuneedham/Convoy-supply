# ConvoySupply — Competitor Analysis

**Date:** 2026-09-20
**Scope:** Semi truck cab organization and storage, US

---

## Method and its limits

Desk research only: search-result scanning, retailer category pages, and marketplace listings. **We have not done systematic price scraping, ad-library review, or mystery shopping.** Treat this as a map of the competitive *structure*, which it establishes well, rather than a precise competitive *dataset*, which it does not.

Two cheap follow-ups would materially improve it, both scheduled in the 30-day plan:

## The competitive structure

Competition arrives in four tiers, and they compete on completely different things:

```
Tier 1  AMAZON MARKETPLACE       Default. Wins on speed + trust + price.
Tier 2  SPECIALTY TRUCK RETAIL   TRUCKiD, RADCO, TruckNTow, Iowa 80. Wins on credibility + range.
Tier 3  TRUCK STOP PHYSICAL      TA/Petro, Pilot, Love's. Wins on immediacy.
Tier 4  DTC BRANDS               Effectively vacant. ← our opening
```

**The finding that matters: Tier 4 is close to empty.** Every other tier is a distribution business. None of them is a brand a driver would name if asked where their cab gear came from.

---

## Tier 1 — Amazon

**What's there [V4]:** deep inventory of semi truck accessories and cab organizers, overwhelmingly generic and white-label. Most trucker accessories sit **under $70**.

**Strengths — and these are real:**
- Two-day delivery. We cannot match this and should stop pretending it's a soft advantage.
- Card on file, one-click, zero friction
- Review volume as trust
- Returns are effortless
- Price pressure from sellers with no brand costs

**Weaknesses:**
- **No brand.** Interchangeable names, no reason to prefer one seller.
- **Photography is uniformly poor** — white background, no context, no cab
- **Listings are keyword soup**, not copy written for a person
- **Products are generic, not purpose-built.** Car organizers sold as truck organizers is a persistent and visible complaint pattern.
- **No systems thinking.** Products are sold one at a time; nothing understands a cab as a whole.
- Review trust is eroding as manipulation becomes obvious to buyers

**How we compete:** not on price, speed, or convenience. On **being specific.** Photographed in an actual Class 8 cab, named for the space it fits, with published specs and a compatibility list. We are the branded alternative in a category that has none.

**How we lose:** by being a slightly nicer generic product at a higher price with slower shipping. If a customer cannot articulate in one sentence why we are different, Amazon wins and deserves to.

---

## Tier 2 — Specialty truck retail

**Who [V4]:** TRUCKiD, RADCO, TruckNTow, Iowa 80 and similar. Cab organizers observed around **$75.99**.

**Strengths:** category authority, breadth, correct fitment, trusted by drivers, decades of relationships.

**Weaknesses:**
- **Retailers, not brands** — they carry other people's products
- Website experience is typically dated, catalog-style, weak on mobile
- **Almost no social media presence in the feed.** We are not fighting them for attention where we advertise.
- No editorial or lifestyle content
- Prices at the top of the range without a premium product experience

**How we compete:** they own search intent; we own the feed. Different battlefield entirely. Their **$75.99** price point also gives us room — our hero at **$54.99** is meaningfully cheaper than specialty retail while sitting well above the Amazon generic, which is exactly the position we want.

---

## Tier 3 — Truck stop retail

TA/Petro, Pilot Flying J, Love's.

**Strengths:** the customer is physically standing there; immediate gratification; no shipping question.

**Weaknesses:** limited selection, captive-audience pricing, quality reputation is poor **[H]**, no organization systems — accessories only.

**How we compete:** we don't, directly. But **truck stop dissatisfaction is a marketing asset.** "You've bought this at a truck stop before. You know how that went." That is an angle, and it is honest.

---

## Tier 4 — DTC brands

**Assessment: effectively vacant.**

Nothing surfaced in this research pass that resembles a direct-to-consumer brand for trucker cab organization with a coherent product line, real photography, and a point of view. Brands exist in adjacent trucking spaces — apparel, chrome, seats — but not here.

**Caveat, stated plainly:** absence of evidence in a desk-research pass is weak evidence of absence. A Meta Ad Library search may reveal an active DTC competitor that search results did not surface. **Do that before committing.** If a well-funded DTC brand is already running this playbook, our whitespace argument weakens considerably and the plan needs revisiting.

## Competitor weaknesses we can actually attack

Ordered by how cheaply we can attack them:

| # | Weakness | Our attack | Cost to us |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Photography is universally bad** | Shoot in a real cab. Before/after. | One day + cab access |
| 2 | **Copy is keyword soup** | Write to a person who knows the job | Free |
| 3 | **Products are generic, not truck-specific** | Name the cabs. Publish dimensions. | Free (research) |
| 4 | **No product system** | Sell a system with a bundle, not a catalog | Free |
| 5 | **No brand** | Have one | Cheap |
| 6 | **Nobody addresses the driver's delivery problem** | Terminal shipping guidance, surfaced early | Free |
| 7 | **Fit uncertainty is unresolved everywhere** | Compatibility list on every PDP | Free (research) |
| 8 | **Fake or manipulated reviews** | Publish none until real ones exist, and say why | Free, and a real differentiator |

**Items 1–8 are almost all free.** That is the whole case for this niche: the competitive gaps are gaps in *effort and taste*, not in capital. A small operator can close them in a week.

## Where we cannot win

Worth being unambiguous so nobody wastes money here:

| Their advantage | Reality |
|---|---|
| **Two-day shipping** | We will not beat Prime. Set expectations honestly instead. |
| **Price** | Sellers with no brand costs will always undercut us. Do not race them. |
| **Review volume** | Years of accumulation. We start at zero and cannot shortcut it ethically. |
| **Range** | 4 SKUs vs. thousands. Reframe as focus, but it is a real constraint. |
| **Trust** | Amazon's returns process is a genuine moat. |

## Price positioning

Observed reference points:

- Specialty retail cab organizer: **$75.99** **[V4]**
- General trucker accessory ceiling: **~$70** **[V4]**
- Amazon generic organizers: **[A]** $15–$40, not verified in this pass
- Marketplace fast-moving shipped band: **$10–$50** **[V1]**

```
$0-15     Generic car organizers (wrong product)
$15-40    Amazon generic "truck" organizers        ← most volume [A]
$40-60    ── CONVOYSUPPLY ($27.99-$54.99) ──       ← target
$60-80    Specialty truck retail ($75.99 observed)
$80+      Installed cab shelving systems
```

**The $40–$60 band is the thinnest part of the market and the most defensible for us.** Above the generics, so we are not fighting on price. Below specialty retail, so there is a rational reason to choose us. And CS-02/CS-03 at $27.99–$29.99 sit inside the Marketplace sweet spot to pull first-time buyers in.

## Differentiation statement

> **Everyone else sells truck accessories. ConvoySupply solves the cab storage problem.**

Operationally that means: purpose-built for Class 8 sleepers, sold as a system, photographed where it is used, spec'd honestly, with no fake reviews and no fake urgency.
