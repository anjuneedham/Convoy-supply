## The selection

> **ConvoySupply sells cab organization and storage gear for people whose vehicle is their workplace — starting with over-the-road truck drivers.**

Beachhead audience: **OTR / long-haul truck drivers** (company drivers and owner-operators).
Adjacent audiences held in reserve: mobile trades running work vans, last-mile and delivery drivers.

Taken from **Niche A** in `niche-analysis.md`.

---

## Why this one, in order of weight

### 1. It is the only candidate that clears every hard constraint at once

The other five each fail at least one structurally:

| Candidate | Fails on |
|---|---|
| Overlanding | Freight cost and bulk (C3, C5); 25–40% category margins **[V5]** |
| Emergency/winter kits | ~10-week season; price war already visible in street pricing **[V6]** |
| Trades / jobsite | **Trademark collision** with Convoy Supply Ltd., a building-materials distributor **[V2]** |
| Pet auto gear | Brand incoherence; vehicle-fit returns |
| Rideshare / delivery | Price-sensitive buyer, thin margins |

Trucker cab gear fails none of them.

### 2. There is a real brand vacuum

This is the strongest commercial argument. The category has **products but no brand.** Search results for semi truck cab organizers return generic white-label listings across Amazon and specialty truck-stop retailers — TRUCKiD, RADCO, TruckNTow — with most accessories priced under $70 and cab organizers observed around $75.99 **[V4]**.

What is absent: any seller with a point of view, consistent photography, a named product line, or a reason to be chosen other than price and Prime shipping.

That is a defensible opening for a small operator, because **it can be attacked with creative quality rather than capital.** We cannot outspend Amazon. We can out-photograph a beige rectangle on a white background.

### 3. Margin structure supports paid acquisition; the alternatives don't

Soft goods (cut-and-sew nylon, webbing, buckles) at a $30–$55 street price is the one structure on the list that plausibly yields **65–75% gross [H]** while staying inside the Marketplace $10–$50 fast-moving band **[V1]**. Overlanding's 25–40% **[V5]** cannot fund a Meta CPA. Emergency kits' component count eats the same room.

Gross margin is the budget for customer acquisition. It is the first thing to check and most of the candidates fail it.

### 4. The audience is large enough and precisely bounded

~3.0–3.1M US truck drivers; ~2.1M heavy/tractor-trailer; 587,000 self-employed owner-operators of whom 351,000 are long-distance **[V3]**.

351,000 long-distance owner-operators is small for a mass-market brand and **more than sufficient** for a test that needs to find a few hundred buyers. Bounded is an advantage: it makes the audience findable and the messaging specific.

### 5. Demographics fit the channel unusually well

Average driver age **47**; owner-operators **56** **[V3]**. That is a Facebook-native cohort, not a TikTok one. Our channel plan — static Facebook posts, Facebook Groups, Facebook Marketplace, Meta feed ads — is aligned with where this buyer actually is, which is a rarer alignment than it sounds. Most "static-image-friendly" niches skew younger and punish us for not making video.

### 6. The brand name finally works for us

"ConvoySupply" supplying the people who drive in convoys is immediately legible. The same name selling dog hammocks or camp stoves is not, and selling roofing materials is a legal problem.

## Target customer (summary — full profile in `customer-avatar.md`)

A 45–60 year old professional driver, most often male, US-based, who spends 200+ nights a year in a sleeper cab. Earns around **$59,710** if a company driver **[V3]**; owner-operators vary far more widely. Identifies as a *professional driver*, is proud of it, and is tired of being marketed to as if he were a hobbyist.

## Core problem

**A sleeper cab is a living space that ships with no storage.**

The truck is engineered for freight, not for the person driving it. There is a bunk, a dash, and a few shallow cubbies. Everything else the driver owns for weeks at a time — clothes, food, tools, paperwork, electronics, chargers, toiletries — has nowhere to live.

The consequences are concrete and daily:
- Gear slides off the bunk under braking
- Items are lost in the same 60 square feet for days
- Paperwork and logbooks get crumpled, wet, or misplaced — with real consequences at a scale or inspection
- Cables tangle across the dash and footwell
- The space feels like a mess, every day, for weeks at a time

This last one matters more than it looks. **This is not a storage problem, it is a dignity problem.** The driver's home is a mess and they cannot fix it with the tools the truck gave them.

## Main buying motivation

Ranked as hypotheses to be tested, strongest first:

1. **Control over a space that currently controls them** — "I want my truck to feel like mine" **[H]**
2. **Stop losing things** — concrete, immediate, unarguable **[H]**
3. **Safety** — loose objects in a cab become projectiles **[H]**
4. **Self-respect / pride in the rig** — the same instinct behind chrome and custom lighting, redirected inward **[H]**
5. **Professional competence** — paperwork you can produce instantly at a weigh station **[H]**

The 30-day ad test exists substantially to find out which of these five actually moves money. See the angle-to-motivation mapping in `marketing/meta-ads.md`.

## Main objections

| Objection | Our answer | Where it's handled |
|---|---|---|
| "Will it fit *my* truck?" | Universal-fit design, published dimensions, named cab compatibility list | PDP fit block + FAQ |
| "I can get this on Amazon tomorrow" | Honest answer: sometimes yes. We compete on build, design, and support — not on speed. We state our real shipping times. | Comparison section, shipping block |
| "This is cheap Chinese junk with a markup" | Specs published: fabric denier, webbing width, stitch type, hardware. Photographed honestly. | PDP specs, `store/product-pages.md` |
| "$55 for a bag?" | Bundle pricing, and a cost-per-month-of-use frame | Offer section |
| "Never heard of you" | No fake reviews, no fake urgency, a real returns policy, a real human replying | Trust section — see below |
| "I'm never home to receive a package" | Ship-to-terminal / ship-to-home-address-for-later options surfaced at checkout | Shipping FAQ |

That last objection is **specific to this niche and most competitors ignore it.** A driver may be 1,800 miles from their delivery address for three weeks. Handling it well is genuine differentiation and costs nothing.

## Product opportunity

Four soft-goods SKUs plus one gated electrical SKU, forming a single coherent system rather than a catalog. Detailed in `research/product-selection.md`.

## Marketing opportunity

The competitive creative is stock photography of products on white backgrounds. Every strong static format is therefore unclaimed here:

- **Before/after cab shots** — the highest-leverage static format available to any niche we evaluated
- **Annotated "where everything lives" diagrams**
- **"Stop doing X" posts** aimed at habits every driver recognizes
- **Honest comparison tables**
- **In-situ photography** that looks like a real truck, not a studio

## Competitive opportunity

See `research/competitor-analysis.md`. In short: the incumbents are strong on **distribution** (Amazon, truck stops) and weak on **everything else** — brand, photography, copy, product naming, customer understanding. We cannot beat distribution. We can beat all of the rest, and in a Facebook feed, distribution is not what decides the click.

## Risks — stated honestly

### R1 — Amazon is the default, and it is genuinely better at some things (HIGH)
Prime delivers in two days. We will not. **Mitigation:** compete where Amazon is weak — brand, design, product-line coherence, and a seller who answers questions. **This risk cannot be eliminated, only outflanked.**

### R2 — We cannot reach drivers efficiently (HIGH)
The audience is mobile and on the road. Meta interest targeting for occupations is inconsistent. **Mitigation:** test broad targeting against interest stacks side by side (`marketing/meta-ads.md`). This is the #1 question the paid test answers.

### R3 — Landed cost comes back worse than modeled (HIGH until quoted)
Every cost figure in this repo is **[A]** until supplier quotes land on Day 4–5. If landed cost on the hero exceeds **$22**, the whole margin structure needs re-pricing. **Mitigation:** quote three suppliers before committing; `finance/unit-economics.md` is built as formulas so a cost change recalculates instead of invalidating.

### R4 — Facebook Marketplace underperforms for shipped auto accessories (MEDIUM)
Auto parts is a named major Marketplace category **[V1]**, but that is category *presence*, not proof of shipped-listing velocity for our specific goods. **Mitigation:** Marketplace is cheap to test — it costs listing time, not ad spend. Run it in parallel and let it prove itself.

### R5 — Trademark exposure (MEDIUM, and time-sensitive)
Convoy Supply Ltd. is a real 1972-founded building-materials distributor **[V2]**. We are in a different category, which helps materially, but "different category" is an argument, not a guarantee. **Mitigation:** USPTO TESS clearance search in the relevant classes **before** spending on logo, packaging, or domain. This is a **Day 1 gate** in `execution/30-day-plan.md`. If clearance is unclear, get 30 minutes of a trademark attorney's time — it is the cheapest risk reduction available anywhere in this plan.

### R6 — Audience is narrow enough to saturate (MEDIUM)
351,000 long-distance owner-operators **[V3]** is a small pond for sustained paid scaling. **Mitigation:** the adjacent audiences (work vans, delivery) are the expansion path, and the brand is deliberately framed as "your vehicle is your workplace" so entering them later is not a repositioning.

### R7 — Seasonality unknown (LOW-MEDIUM)
**[A]** Plausible that cab-outfitting purchases cluster at new-truck acquisition and around winter. Not verified. Note it; don't plan around it yet.

## What would make this test fail

Not "what are we worried about" — **what specifically constitutes failure.** Written before the test so it cannot be rationalized after it.

The test has failed if, after 30 days and the budget in `finance/testing-budget.md`:

1. **CPA exceeds break-even CPA across all creative angles and both targeting approaches**, with no angle showing a downward CPA trend over its last 7 days.
2. **Landing page view → add-to-cart stays under 3%** across 1,000+ LPVs. That means the offer or the audience is wrong, not the ad.
3. **CTR stays below 0.8%** across all 10 creative concepts. That means the audience does not recognize the problem in the way we described it.
4. **Marketplace produces zero sales from 30+ listings across 21 days.** The channel is not viable for these goods.
5. **Landed cost + shipping leaves contribution margin below 45%** at target price. There is no advertising budget to be had.
6. **Refund/complaint rate exceeds 8%** on the first 50 orders. The product is not good enough, and everything else is irrelevant.

Any **two** of these = stop and reassess. Numbers 5 or 6 alone = stop immediately.

## What evidence would cause us to pivot — and to what

| Evidence | Pivot |
|---|---|
| Ads work, but only for the **cargo net / low-priced SKUs** | Re-price down and move to a volume-and-bundle model |
| Ads fail, but **Marketplace sells steadily** | Marketplace-first business; Shopify becomes the fulfillment back end, not the storefront |
| **Work-van and trades buyers** convert better than OTR drivers | Shift beachhead to mobile trades — but re-check R5 trademark exposure first, since that moves us toward Convoy Supply Ltd.'s category |
| Strong **CTR, weak conversion** | Problem is the offer/page, not the market. Rebuild the landing page before touching the audience. |
| Strong conversion, **CPA above break-even** | Raise AOV: push bundles, add a fifth SKU, test a higher-priced hero |
| Nothing works at all across every angle | The hypothesis "truck drivers will buy cab gear from an unknown brand on Facebook" is false. Return to `niche-analysis.md` and take **Niche C or E** with the learnings intact. |

## Explicitly: this is a hypothesis

The market research says this niche is *plausible*. It does not say it will work. Nothing in this repository should be read as a prediction of profitability, and the unit economics are deliberately written as formulas rather than conclusions so that real numbers replace estimates without anyone having to rewrite the reasoning.
