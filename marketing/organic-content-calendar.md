# ConvoySupply — 30-Day Organic Content Calendar

**Date:** 2026-09-20
**Channels:** Facebook Page (primary) · Instagram (secondary, cross-posted) · Facebook Groups (participation, not posting)
**Cadence:** 5 posts/week, Mon–Fri. Weekends off — engagement is lower and you need the time.
**Format:** static images and carousels only. No video required.

---

## Channel weighting

Roughly **70% Facebook, 20% Marketplace, 10% Instagram** — from the avatar research, where average driver age is **47** and owner-operators **56** **[V3]**. Instagram is cross-posted because Meta makes it nearly free, not because the audience lives there. If Instagram shows unexpected traction by Day 20, revisit — but do not plan around it.

## Posting times

| Slot | Time (ET) | Why **[H]** |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | 6:00–7:00am | Pre-trip. Phone in hand, coffee, sitting still. |
| Secondary | 8:00–9:30pm | Parked for the night. The longest scroll window of the day. |

**[H] — untested.** Meta's own audience insights will beat this guess within two weeks. Treat the schedule as a starting point and let the data move it.

## The 4:1 rule

**Four non-selling posts for every selling post.** This audience scrolls past brand feeds that sell constantly. Education, problem recognition, and community posts are what earn the right to the occasional promotion — and they are also what generate saves and shares, which are the strongest organic signals available.

## Group strategy — participate, don't post

Trucking Facebook Groups are high-trust and **hostile to overt advertising** **[H]**. The rule for the first 30 days:

- **Do not post promotional content in any group. Ever.**
- Comment helpfully on organization and setup threads, with no link
- Answer "what should I get for my cab" questions with genuine advice, including products that are not ours
- Post the measuring guide (E-03) **only** where a group explicitly permits useful resources, and only after being an actual participant
- The Page link in the profile does the selling. Nothing else does.

Getting banned from the three biggest groups in week one costs more than any post could earn.

## Daily operating routine

**Morning (15 min)** — post the scheduled item (or confirm the scheduler fired) · reply to overnight comments and DMs · check Marketplace messages.
**Evening (20 min)** — reply to all comments · spend 10 minutes participating helpfully in one Group, no links · log the day in `execution/daily-log.md`.

**Response-time commitment: under 2 hours in waking hours.** On a Page with no reviews and no history, reply speed *is* the trust signal.

## Weekly review (Fridays, 30 min)

1. Pull reach, engagement rate, saves, shares, comments, link clicks into `execution/metrics.md`
2. Identify the best and worst post of the week and write down **why** — not just which
3. Any post outperforming by 3× or more becomes an ad creative candidate (`marketing/meta-ads.md`)
4. Adjust next week's mix if a category is clearly out- or under-performing

## What to watch for

| Signal | Read | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Educational posts out-save product posts | Audience wants help before it wants product | Shift the mix toward education; it is working |
| Problem posts get comments, product posts get nothing | Problem recognized, offer not landing | The issue is the offer or the page, not the audience |
| High reach, no clicks | Content is entertaining, not motivating | Strengthen CTAs; check the link destination |
| Comments are corrections from drivers | **Good sign, and act on it** | We got a detail wrong. Fix it publicly and thank them. |
| Group comments generate DMs | Group participation is working | Do more of it — still without posting promotionally |

That fourth row matters more than it looks. A driver correcting a detail in public is engagement *and* free product research, and handling it gracefully in front of an audience is worth more than any post we could plan.

## Rules

1. Never post promotional content in a Facebook Group
2. Never repost a customer photo without explicit permission
3. Never fabricate a review, testimonial, or engagement
4. Never buy followers or engagement
5. Never argue with a critic in public — answer once, honestly, then move to DM
6. Never post a spec figure that a supplier spec sheet doesn't support
7. Every claim in every caption must be true and checkable
