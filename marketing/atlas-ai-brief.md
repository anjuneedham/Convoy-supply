# PART 1 — WHAT THIS BUSINESS IS

## The one-liner
Purpose-built storage and organization gear for the cab of a Class 8 semi truck.

## Positioning statement
For professional drivers who live out of their cab, ConvoySupply makes purpose-built storage and organization gear that turns a truck's dead space into a system. Unlike generic car organizers and unbranded marketplace listings, everything is chosen around the actual dimensions and daily reality of a sleeper cab.

## The category
**Not "truck accessories."** That is a parts-counter category and it puts us next to chrome bumpers and air horns.

**"Cab systems."** The cab is a workspace and a living space, and it can be set up well or badly. Accessories compete on price; systems compete on completeness. A system justifies a bundle. A bundle raises average order value. Average order value is what survives a high cost per acquisition.

## The customer
- Over-the-road (OTR) long-haul truck drivers, primarily US
- Live in a 50–70 sq ft sleeper cab for 2–3 weeks at a time
- Their cab is simultaneously office, bedroom, and kitchen
- The truck ships with a bunk, a dash, and shallow cubbies — that's the entire storage provision
- Everything else ends up on the passenger seat, the bunk, or the floor
- **Highly suspicious of marketing.** They discuss sellers in Facebook Groups and will publicly mock fake urgency, fake reviews, and fake scarcity
- Frequently browsing on a phone, in a parking lot, on poor cellular signal

## Brand promise
**Everything in its place. Nothing in your way.**

What we commit to:
1. **Built for the actual space.** Real dimensions published.
2. **No installation, ever.** Straps, hangs, sits. Nothing drills or bolts. *(This matters commercially: company drivers whose carriers prohibit vehicle modifications can still buy.)*
3. **Honest specs.** Material, dimensions, weight — published, including where we're not best in class.
4. **Honest marketing.** No fake reviews, no fake countdowns, no invented scarcity.
5. **A real person answers.**

**What we explicitly do NOT promise:** lowest price, fastest shipping, or a lifetime warranty. We will not beat Amazon Prime and will not pretend to.

## Tagline
**Squared away.** — trucking and military vernacular for *organized, correct, in order*. Signals insider knowledge to the audience, reads as plain English to everyone else.

## What this means for the page design

**Bundles carry nearly double the cost-per-acquisition headroom of single items ($37–41 vs $18–23).**

In a category where a click costs $1–2, a page that sells only single items is fighting uphill. A page that converts visitors into bundle buyers has real room to pay for traffic.

**Therefore the page must:**
1. Show single products clearly (that's what people search for and click on)
2. Present the bundle as the obvious upgrade immediately after — not buried at the bottom
3. Make the saving concrete and honest ($18.98 off, against real prices actually charged)
4. Never use a fake compare-at price — the only legitimate compare-at is the true sum of individual prices

**CS-04 carries the thinnest margin (37%, below our 45% floor).** Do not build the page or any ad around CS-04 as a standalone hero. It belongs inside the Full Rig Kit, where the other three products carry the blended average.

# PART 4 — CHANNEL STRATEGY AND WHAT EACH DEMANDS OF THE PAGE

**This is the most important section for page architecture.**

The plan runs channels in strict sequence: **Google first → Facebook/Instagram second → TikTok third.** Each stage sends a fundamentally different visitor to the same page. The page must serve them in that priority order.

## Stage 1 — GOOGLE (live now, the page must be optimized for this)

**Who arrives:** Someone who typed "truck cab organizer" into Google. They have already decided they want this category of thing. They are comparing options and checking whether you're legitimate.

**What they need, in order:**
1. **Immediate confirmation they're in the right place** — the product, visible, above the fold, no scrolling
2. **Price, immediately.** Hiding price to "build value" loses this visitor instantly
3. **Add to cart, reachable without scrolling** on mobile
4. **Trust signals fast** — returns policy, shipping reality, the fact that a human answers. They've never heard of this brand.
5. **The bundle as an obvious upgrade** — this is where the margin is

**What they do NOT need:** a long problem-agitation story. They already know they have the problem; that's why they searched. Making a Google visitor scroll through three screens of "does your cab feel cluttered?" before showing the product loses the sale.

**Page requirement:** **Product, price, and CTA above the fold. Bundle within the first scroll. Trust strip immediately after.**

## Stage 2 — FACEBOOK / INSTAGRAM (next, after Google produces conversion data)

**Who arrives:** Someone scrolling a feed who was stopped by an image. They were not looking for this. They may not have consciously registered that they have this problem.

**What they need:**
1. A **problem statement they recognize** — "your passenger seat is where everything ends up"
2. **Before/after visual** — the single highest-converting asset for this product category
3. Proof the brand is real and honest
4. Then the product and price

**Page requirement:** These problem/story sections must **exist on the page, positioned below the product-and-price block.** A Google visitor scrolls past them; a Facebook visitor scrolls into them. Same page, different entry mindset, both served.

## Stage 3 — TIKTOK (last)

**Who arrives:** The coldest traffic of the three, on mobile, with the shortest attention span, often with sound on.

**What they need:**
1. Instant visual payoff — the product doing the thing, immediately
2. A **persistent, always-reachable CTA** — they will not scroll back up
3. Extremely fast load on mobile data

**Page requirement:** **A sticky mobile add-to-cart bar** that appears on scroll and never leaves. This serves TikTok most, but helps all three channels.

## The resulting page architecture

Build the homepage in exactly this order. The sequence is the strategy.

| # | Section | Serves | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hero** — headline, one-line value prop, primary CTA | Google, all | Confirms the visitor is in the right place in under 2 seconds |
| 2 | **Product grid** — all 4 with photo, name, price, add-to-cart | **Google (critical)** | The searcher sees the thing and the price immediately |
| 3 | **Bundle block** — Cab Reset Kit + Full Rig Kit, honest savings | **Google (margin)** | Where the CPA headroom lives; must be early, not buried |
| 4 | **Trust strip** — no installation, honest shipping, 30-day returns, no fake reviews | Google, all | Unknown brand objection handling |
| 5 | **Problem section** — "your passenger seat is where everything ends up" | **Facebook/Instagram** | Cold traffic needs this; Google traffic scrolls past harmlessly |
| 6 | **How it works / before-after** | Facebook, TikTok | Visual proof |
| 7 | **Honest social proof** — "no reviews yet, we won't invent any" | All | Converts a weakness into the most credible thing on the page |
| 8 | **FAQ** | All | Objection handling, plus SEO value for Google |
| 9 | **Final CTA** | All | Catches the deep scroller |
| — | **Sticky mobile CTA bar** (appears after section 2) | **TikTok, all mobile** | Persistent conversion path |

**The core principle: Google's visitor must be able to buy without ever scrolling past section 3. Everything from section 5 down exists for the social traffic that comes later.**

# PART 5 — BRAND VOICE (non-negotiable)

**Direct. Specific. Competent. Never salesy.** Write like a good supplier talking to a professional who knows the job and whose time is worth something.

## Rules

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Short sentences, concrete nouns | Adjective stacks ("premium ultra-durable heavy-duty") |
| Real numbers — 31" × 21", $50, 5–11 days | Vague claims — "super strong," "huge capacity" |
| Admit tradeoffs | Pretend there are none |
| "Professional driver" or "driver" | "Trucker" in formal copy |
| Plain words | *elevate · unlock · game-changer · revolutionary · seamlessly · curated · ultimate · must-have · hack* |

## Banned phrases
elevate · unlock · game-changer · revolutionary · seamlessly · curated · premium (standalone) · ultimate · must-have · life-changing · hack · obsessed · **king of the road · road warrior · knight of the highway** (the last three read as patronizing to this audience)

## The voice test
Read it aloud. **Would a driver with 19 years behind the wheel believe this was written by someone who has sat in a sleeper?** If the sentence could equally sell a phone case, rewrite it.

## Calibration

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "Elevate your cab with our premium organizational solution!" | "Your passenger seat is not a shelf. This is." |
| "Super durable heavy-duty construction" | "PU leather, 4-point strap mount, sits flat when loaded." |
| "Limited time offer, act now!" | "$50. Free shipping over $75." |
| "Customers love it!" | "We launched this month. No reviews yet — yours would be the first." |

That last row is the whole brand in one line.

# PART 6 — VISUAL IDENTITY

## Color palette

| Role | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Asphalt (primary dark) | `#1C1F21` | Backgrounds, header, hero |
| Steel (secondary dark) | `#3A4247` | Cards, borders |
| **Hi-viz amber (accent)** | `#E8B23A` | **CTAs, highlights — this is the one accent color** |
| Concrete (light bg) | `#EDEBE7` | Section backgrounds — warm, not clinical white |
| Paper (lightest) | `#FAF9F7` | Page background |
| Ink (body text) | `#14171A` | Text on light |
| Muted | `#6B7075` | Secondary text |

**Why hi-viz amber:** borrowed from the safety-equipment vocabulary this audience sees every day — vests, placards, warning triangles. It reads as *work*, not *retail*.

**Deliberately avoided:** chrome and gradients (dated trucking cliché), red-white-and-blue (crowded and politically loaded), pastels, neon.

## Typography
- **Headlines:** Barlow Condensed, 600/700, uppercase. Condensed grotesque with transport-signage lineage.
- **Body:** Inter, 400/500/600. Highly legible on mobile.
- **Specs/numbers:** Roboto Mono, 400. Monospace signals technical data.

## Layout principles
- **Generous whitespace.** The brand is about uncluttered space — a cluttered site contradicts the product.
- **Hard edges.** 2px border radius maximum. Rounded corners read consumer-soft.
- **Left-aligned body text.** Centered paragraphs read as marketing.
- **Specs in tables**, monospace, not prose.

## Logo
Wordmark only, no icon: **CONVOY** in light/dark + **SUPPLY** in hi-viz amber. Barlow Condensed bold, uppercase. Optional strapline beneath in mono: SQUARED AWAY.

# PART 7 — ABSOLUTE PROHIBITIONS

These are not preferences. A page that violates any of these does not ship.

1. **No countdown timers.** None, anywhere, for any reason.
2. **No fake stock counters** ("Only 3 left!") unless literally true and system-driven.
3. **No "X people are viewing this."**
4. **No fabricated reviews, ratings, testimonials, or customer quotes.** We have zero reviews. The page says so.
5. **No fake compare-at pricing.** The only legitimate compare-at is the true sum of individual product prices on a bundle.
6. **No exit-intent discount popups. No spin-to-win.**
7. **No fabricated press mentions, awards, or "as seen in."**
8. **No claiming fast shipping.** Real transit is 5–11 days after a 1–3 day processing window. Say the real number.
9. **No invented product specs.** Materials and dimensions are listed in Part 2. Nothing beyond that is known. Do not add load ratings, denier counts, or warranty terms.
10. **No compliance, safety, medical, or legal claims** — especially on CS-03. "Organizes documents," never "helps you pass inspection."

**Why this matters commercially, not just ethically:** this audience discusses sellers in Facebook Groups and will publicly mock a fake countdown timer. The only real asset a brand-new store has is being visibly honest in a category that visibly is not.

## The honest-social-proof block — use close to verbatim

> **No reviews yet.**
>
> We launched this month. We're not going to put fake ones here, and we're not going to buy any.
>
> If you're one of the first people to try this, tell us what's wrong with it. We'd rather fix the product than pad the page.

## The Amazon objection — answer it, don't duck it

> **Can I get this cheaper on Amazon?**
>
> Probably, yes. And it'll get there faster.
>
> What you'll get is a generic listing with truck keywords in the title, from a seller who changes their name every six months. Ours is chosen and described for a Class 8 cab, the dimensions are published, and if it doesn't fit you send it back within 30 days. If two-day shipping matters more than fit, Amazon is the right call and we'd rather you were happy than out $50.

- Homepage under 1.2 MB total
- Largest Contentful Paint under 2.5s on 4G

- Sticky add-to-cart bar after the product section
- Every section collapses to single column under 600px

- Featured product images must contain **no promotional text overlays** — Google disapproves these and removes the product from Shopping

- Three supplier images carry promotional text overlays — usable on the site, never as the featured image
